№1

The Culture

two chemicals · no plan · Turing, 1952

Two invisible chemicals drift across a grid. A feeds freely; B eats it and multiplies. That is the entire rulebook. And yet strip away the feed and the kill and all that is left is a preference for patterns that should not exist — stripes, spirals, cell walls, the crackle of dried mud, a leopard's shoulder.

Turing wrote this down in 1952 to explain a question nobody had answered: why is a cow patched? The answer cost him his final university paper. It also turned out to be the reason no two fingerprints match, the reason giraffes are patchy, and the reason you can grow bacteria in a dish and watch them invent geometry.

∂A/∂t = DA∇²A − AB² + F(1−A)
∂B/∂t = DB∇²B + AB² − (F+k)B

Feed rate F and kill rate k are the only two knobs. Drag on the plate and you are injecting B by hand — a tiny act of god.

The Dreamer

a neural network · written by hand · knows nothing

Draw anything. A square, your initials, a face — the network has never seen it before and will never see it again. It starts from pure noise every single time, guesses, measures how badly it did, and corrects itself by hand-writing the derivative of its own mistake.

There is no framework here. No tensor library, no autodiff engine, no optimiser, no build step. Just three functions — forward, backward, step — and 168 of the 1024 points handed to it at a time, chosen at random. The weights are stored output-major so that every inner loop walks memory in a straight line; switch that round and the arithmetic is identical but 40% slower. The buffers are allocated once and refilled forever, because handing the garbage collector half a megabyte sixty times a second costs more than all the mathematics.

The grid is not decoration. It is what the neurons actually compute when asked how much, here, at this x and this y? A hidden unit has no idea what a drawing is — it has never seen one, and it will forget the moment the weights move. Yet within a few hundred steps each one settles on a private opinion about the entire plane: some find edges, some find blobs, and one of them will always, inexplicably, find the diagonal.

The Light

no meshes · no triangles · no textures · only photons

Nothing in this image exists. There is no model, no polygon, no photograph, no light map. A fragment shader fires a photon out of the lamp in a random direction and asks one question over and over: what do you hit? Then it obeys — bouncing off the walls, refracting through the glass, bending toward the polished sphere — and reports home.

Then ask it again, and again, and average the answers. The running count is printed in the corner; on an ordinary graphics card it climbs past forty million paths every second, and the flat white floor quietly turns pink where the red wall reflects into it. The shadow under the sphere is soft because nobody told it to be. Every image here arrived by being found.

The Weather

incompressible · swirling · Stam, 1999

A window of air, fifty-eight thousand cells wide, and one rule: never compress. Push it here and it must go there. Every frame each cell asks its neighbours which way the wind is already blowing, works out how far it has been squeezed, and then tries very hard to un-squeeze itself.

That's the whole method — twenty-eight sweeps of subtraction, which sounds like nothing at all. And yet by the time the correction is finished there is a front, an eddy behind it, a plume that rolls over on itself and keeps rolling. Stir it with your hand and it will remember your hand for a while, because it is not storing your input. It is storing the consequences.

Everything here is the source.

There are no dependencies. No framework, no bundler, no image files, no web fonts beyond two, no analytics. Open the repository and every line that produced this page is right there — the simulation kernels, the GLSL, the backward pass, the pressure solver.

The honest summary is that this is all arithmetic, run patiently. A cell of chemical. A gradient. A photon, sampled. A neighbour's velocity. Give any of them enough time and enough copies and something that looks designed walks out of the arithmetic.

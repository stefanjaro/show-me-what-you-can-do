The house that is not a house.

Behind this page a 3D renderer written in plain JavaScript is drawing a building that cannot exist. Every wall, every texture, every shadow comes out of arithmetic — there is nothing here but code. Walk into it.

Four ways to break a building.

Each wing of the house breaks geometry in a different way. None of them use a trick: the walls are solid, the rooms are real, and the doorways are the only thing that is lying to you.

WING I

The Hall of Turning

Four rooms in a ring, three ordinary doors and one twisted a quarter turn. Walk the loop and come back facing the wrong way. The ring carries holonomy — a property of curved space, built out of flat ones.

WING II

The Penrose Ascent

Twelve steps to a flight, four flights to a circuit, and the top of every circuit is the bottom of the next — one landing higher than the last time you stood there. Climb as long as you like.

WING III

The Recessional

Seven rooms, each four fifths of the last, each turned a quarter. The far wall is always the same distance away. The eye never reaches the end, and neither does the renderer, which recurses until it runs out of depth.

WING IV

The Inverted Chapel

A doorway cut high in the vault, hinged about nothing. Step through and the floor goes away: you stand on the ceiling, the pews hang below you, and the seal is out of reach unless you are.

Everything here is arithmetic.

No engine, no WebGL, no image files, no models, no audio files. What follows is what the page is actually doing.

The renderer

  • Portal culling. Rooms are convex. A doorway is a window into the next room; the renderer walks the graph of rooms, clipping each view to the cone it can see through.
  • Z-buffered rasteriser. Perspective-correct texture mapping, typed-array spans, per-pixel depth.
  • Coloured lighting. Per-vertex wrapped diffuse from point lights with inverse-square falloff, plus distance fog.
  • Sprites. Additive billboards for flame, dust and the seals.

The portal algebra

  • Gate frames. A doorway is a rectangle with an origin, two in-plane axes and an outward normal.
  • The gluing. X = [−uB, vB, −nB]·[uA, vA, nA]T — the identity for two doors back to back, a quarter turn for a bent one.
  • Twists. An extra rotation about the far door's own frame. Half a turn each way stands you on the ceiling without turning you round.
  • Verification. 82 headless checks: every gluing is a proper rotation, every room is reachable, and the visitor is walked through all four impossibilities.

Everything else

  • Textures. Value noise, fBm, ridged multifractals, Worley cells and domain warping, written from scratch, generating stone, marble, oak, tile, gilt, tapestry, coffering and the void.
  • Sound. A drone of three detuned saws, wind, torch crackle, footsteps, a bell for each seal, and the noise a doorway makes when it bends.
  • Movement. Sub-stepped collision against six planes per room, with doorways tested as rectangles in the door's own frame.
  • Adaptive quality. The renderer trades resolution for frame rate and settles on its own.
